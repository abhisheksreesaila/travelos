The fun facts the trip matrix shows while its squares load (`gitaway/placefacts.py`). Each was checked on 2026-11-12 against the page given, fetched that day;
the quote is the page's own words (passages joined with "..."). Sources are official where we could find one (NPS, US Forest Service, Hawaii DLNR, the City

## San Diego

- San Diego has about 70 miles of coastline, lined with one beach town after another.
  - Source: https://www.sandiego.org/coastal-neighborhoods
  - Says: "70 miles of beautiful coastline set the stage for a collection of beach communities"
- Juan Rodriguez Cabrillo led the first European trip along the West Coast and sailed into San Diego Bay.
  - Source: https://www.nps.gov/cabr/learn/historyculture/juan-rodriguez-cabrillo.htm
  - Says: "Juan Rodriguez Cabrillo led the first European expedition that explored what is now the west coast of the United States. ... arrived at 'a very good enclosed port,' which is known today as San Diego Bay."
- Gray whales swim past San Diego mostly in late December to February, at about 5 miles per hour.
  - Source: https://www.nps.gov/cabr/learn/nature/whale-watching.htm
  - Says: "Most pass San Diego in late December, January, and February. ... Expect them to move at a steady speed of four or five knots, or about five miles per hour."
- Balboa Park began in 1868, when city leaders set aside 1,400 acres of land for a park.
  - Source: https://www.balboapark.org/about/
  - Says: "Balboa Park began as 1400 acres of land set aside in 1868 by San Diego civic leaders."

## Los Angeles

- The Hollywood Sign first read HOLLYWOODLAND, an ad for new houses, and was meant to last only a year and a half.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Sign
  - Says: "originally read "HOLLYWOODLAND" to promote the name of a new housing development [8] ... intended to last only a year and a half. [14]"
- Griffith Park began in 1896 as a Christmas gift to the people of LA: 3,015 acres of land.
  - Source: https://griffithobservatory.lacity.gov/visit/griffith-park/
  - Says: "bequeathed 3,015 acres (equal to five square miles) of his Rancho Los Feliz estate ... as a Christmas gift to the people of Los Angeles to be used as parkland"
- Griffith Park has a 53-mile network of trails, fire roads and bridle paths to explore.
  - Source: https://griffithobservatory.lacity.gov/visit/griffith-park/
  - Says: "Hikers may use the entire 53-mile network of trails, fire roads, and bridle paths"
- At the La Brea Tar Pits, scientists dig up Ice Age fossils every day, right in the middle of the city.
  - Source: https://tarpits.org/la-brea-tar-pits-history
  - Says: "The only active paleontological research site in the world that is located in a major urban area ... where excavation continues daily"

## New York

- To reach the Statue of Liberty's crown from the ground, you climb 377 steps.
  - Source: https://www.nps.gov/stli/learn/statue-of-liberty-facts.htm
  - Says: "From the ground, visitors climb 377 steps to reach the crown."
- The Statue of Liberty is made of copper. It turned green all by itself as the metal aged in the air.
  - Source: https://www.nps.gov/stli/learn/statue-of-liberty-facts.htm
  - Says: "The copper turned green naturally due to oxidation."
- The Empire State Building went up in just 13 months, from 1930 to 1931.
  - Source: https://www.esbnyc.com/about/facts-figures
  - Says: "Among NYC's five tallest buildings, the Empire State Building is the oldest, built in just 13 months from 1930 to 1931."
- Central Park has 843 acres, about 10,000 benches, and more than 210 kinds of birds visit each year.
  - Source: https://www.centralparknyc.org/your-park
  - Says: "843 acres. 210 bird species. 10,000 benches. Eight running loops. ... More than 210 bird species make an appearance in Central Park each year."

## Lake Tahoe

- Lake Tahoe is 1,645 feet deep at its deepest point.
  - Source: https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/laketahoebasin/about-area
  - Says: "The deepest recorded depth of Lake Tahoe is 1,645 feet."
- Tahoe holds enough water to cover all of California, if it were flat, about 14 inches deep.
  - Source: https://www.keeptahoeblue.org/abouttahoe
  - Says: "The water contained in Lake Tahoe is enough to cover a flat area the size of California to a depth of 14 inches."
- Lake Tahoe is the second deepest lake in America, after Oregon's Crater Lake.
  - Source: https://www.keeptahoeblue.org/abouttahoe
  - Says: "It is the second deepest lake in America, next to Oregon's Crater Lake."
- Tahoe is 22 miles long and 12 miles wide, with 72 miles of shoreline to drive around.
  - Source: https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/laketahoebasin/about-area
  - Says: "Tahoe is 22 miles long, North to South, and 12 miles wide, with 72 miles of shoreline."

## Orlando

- Downtown's Lake Eola began as a sinkhole on cattle rancher Jacob Summerlin's land in the 1870s.
  - Source: https://orlando.gov/Parks-the-Environment/Directory/Lake-Eola-Park/Lake-Eola-History
  - Says: "Jacob Summerlin the "Cattle King of Florida" arrived in 1873 to purchase 200 acres of land ... Shortly after the purchase, on the edge of his property there was a sink hole forming."
- The tiered fountain in the middle of Lake Eola was built in 1957.
  - Source: https://orlando.gov/Parks-the-Environment/Directory/Lake-Eola-Park/Lake-Eola-History
  - Says: "This tiered fountain at the center of Lake Eola was built in 1957 and refurbished in 2011."
- Gatorland, the "Alligator Capital of the World," opened as a roadside attraction in 1949.
  - Source: https://www.blogs.gatorland.com/about.shtml
  - Says: "The park opened as a roadside attraction in 1949. ... Known internationally as the "Alligator Capital of the World ®,""
- Gatorland is home to rare white alligators, called leucistic, among thousands of gators and crocs.
  - Source: https://www.blogs.gatorland.com/about.shtml
  - Says: "showcasing thousands of alligators and crocodiles including four extremely rare leucistic "white" alligators."

  - Source: https://visitpalmsprings.com/media/media-kit/
  - Says: "The Palm Springs Aerial Tramway is the world’s largest rotating tram car"
- The tram's mountain station sits at 8,516 feet, about 30 degrees cooler than the desert floor.
  - Source: https://visitpalmsprings.com/media/media-kit/
  - Says: "The Mountain Station is at 8,516 feet ... about 30 degrees cooler than the desert floor"
- Palm Canyon is considered the world's largest California fan palm oasis.
  - Source: https://visitpalmsprings.com/media/media-kit/
  - Says: "Palm Canyon is considered the world’s largest California Fan Palm Oasis."
- The Cahuilla people have lived in Tahquitz Canyon for at least 5,000 years.
  - Source: https://visitpalmsprings.com/media/media-kit/
  - Says: "Archaeological research has discovered that the Cahuilla have occupied Tahquitz Canyon for at least 5,000 years."

## Santa Barbara

- Stearns Wharf was finished in 1872 and is California's oldest working wharf.
  - Source: https://santabarbaraca.com/businesses/stearns-wharf/
  - Says: "it is California’s oldest working wharf ... The Wharf was completed in 1872"
- The Santa Barbara Mission, founded in 1786, is nicknamed the "Queen of the Missions" for its beauty.
  - Source: https://santabarbaraca.com/businesses/old-mission-santa-barbara/
  - Says: "Known as “Queen of the Missions” for its exceptional beauty ... the Santa Barbara Mission was founded by the Spanish Franciscans in 1786"
- Each year artists cover the Mission's plaza in giant chalk paintings at the I Madonnari festival.
  - Source: https://santabarbaraca.com/businesses/old-mission-santa-barbara/
  - Says: "enjoy stunning outdoor artwork during the annual I Madonnari Street Painting Festival. ... Artists transform the pavement of the Mission Plaza with 150 vibrant, large-scale pastel chalk masterpieces"
- Offshore, the Channel Islands' island fox is about the size of a house cat and lives nowhere else on Earth.
  - Source: https://www.nps.gov/chis/learn/nature/island-fox.htm
  - Says: "At 12 to 13 inches in height and 4 to 5 pounds, the island fox is about the size of a housecat. ... they are found nowhere else on Earth."

## Orange County

- Disneyland opened on July 17, 1955, the only Disney park built under Walt Disney's own watch.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland
  - Says: "the only one designed and constructed under the direct supervision of Walt Disney [1] ... opening on July 17, 1955 [1]"
- The boysenberry was bred by Walter Knott's friend Rudolph Boysen, and named after him.
  - Source: https://www.nps.gov/home/learn/historyculture/walter-knott.htm
  - Says: "Walter Knott began to work with a friend, Rudolph Boysen, to develop a cross between red raspberries and blackberries. ... he named the new mixture "boysenberry" after his friend."
- George Freeth, the Father of Modern Surfing, surfed Huntington Beach the day its concrete pier opened in 1914.
  - Source: https://www.surfcityusa.com/history/
  - Says: "now known as the Father of Modern Surfing ... Freeth gave the first surfing demonstration in town, appropriately on the same day the new concrete pier opened in 1914"
- Huntington Beach's nickname Surf City comes from a 1960s hit song by Jan and Dean.
  - Source: https://www.surfcityusa.com/history/
  - Says: "the popular duo Jan and Dean released their hit song of the same name"

## Honolulu

- Iolani Palace in Honolulu is the only official royal residence in the United States.
  - Source: https://www.iolanipalace.org/
  - Says: "the only official royal residence in the United States"
- Diamond Head got its name from sailors who thought they found diamonds. They were shiny calcite crystals.
  - Source: https://www.gohawaii.com/islands/oahu/regions/honolulu/leahi-diamond-head
  - Says: "These "diamonds" were actually shiny calcite crystals that had no value."
- Diamond Head's Hawaiian name is Le'ahi, meaning "brow of the tuna" in English.
  - Source: https://www.gohawaii.com/islands/oahu/regions/honolulu/leahi-diamond-head
  - Says: "Known as Lēʻahi (brow of the tuna) in Hawaiian"
- The Diamond Head summit trail, built in 1908, climbs 560 feet and passes through a lit 225-foot tunnel.
  - Source: https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/dsp/hiking/oahu/diamond-head-summit-trail/
  - Says: "built in 1908 as part of Oʻahu’s coastal defense system ... climbs 560 feet (171 m) in elevation ... The ascent continues up steep stairs and through a lighted 225-foot tunnel."

## Yosemite

- Yosemite Falls drops 2,425 feet in three parts, making it one of the world's tallest waterfalls.
  - Source: https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/waterfalls.htm
  - Says: "Yosemite Falls, one of the world's tallest, is actually made up of three separate falls: ... 2,425 feet / 740 m"
- El Capitan rises over 3,000 feet above the valley floor, and Half Dome nearly 5,000.
  - Source: https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/formations.htm
  - Says: "El Capitan, rising over 3,000 feet above the floor of Yosemite Valley, is a favorite for experienced rock climbers. ... Rising nearly 5,000 feet above the Valley floor, it is one of the most sought-after landmarks in Yosemite."
- In mid to late February, Horsetail Fall can glow orange at sunset, as if the waterfall were on fire.
  - Source: https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/waterfalls.htm
  - Says: "Horsetail Fall is famous for appearing to be on fire when it reflects the orange glow of sunset in mid- to late February."
- Mariposa Grove has over 500 giant sequoias, including the Grizzly Giant, about 3,000 years old.
  - Source: https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/mg.htm
  - Says: "is home to over 500 mature giant sequoias ... the 3,000-year-old Grizzly Giant"
