# Routes and drive times for commercial use (F-204)

Researched 2026-11-07 against each provider's own pricing and terms pages, the routing engines' own docs and source, and Geofabrik's download pages. Every claim links to the page it came from. **[unverified]** marks things the sources did not state outright; **[derived]** marks arithmetic on a published number. The HeiGIT, HERE, TomTom and Hetzner pages only render with JavaScript, so they were read with headless Chromium the same day. This is a careful reading of public terms, not legal advice.

## TL;DR

- **The router we use today does not allow our use.** The OSRM demo server is "restricted to reasonable, non-commercial use-cases. Do not exceed 1 request per second" ([OSRM wiki](https://github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/wiki/Demo-server)). Its operator, FOSSGIS, allows commercial use only "if the use of the services does not constitute a substantial part of an online offering" ([FOSSGIS policy](https://fossgis.de/arbeitsgruppen/osm-server/nutzungsbedingungen/)). Drive times and route lines are a core part of the day plan. Separately, `geo.py` has `OSRM_GATE = Gate(0.25)`: up to four requests a second against a stated limit of one.
- **Recommendation: openrouteservice (HeiGIT), Standard plan.** It costs €0 and is "Free to use for everyone": 2,000 directions a day, 40 a minute ([plans](https://account.heigit.org/info/plans)). Results "in any context are licensed under CC-BY-SA 4.0" ([terms](https://account.heigit.org/info/tos)), so we may cache legs forever as long as we show "© openrouteservice by HeiGIT | Data from OpenStreetMap". It covers the whole world, with car and walking profiles. A few thousand new legs a month is about 5% of the cap **[derived]**. The work is one account, one key in `ORS_API_KEY`, a credit line, and a small change to `geo.py` (§5).
- **Fallback: Geoapify Free.** 3,000 credits a day, and a two-point route costs 1 credit. "You can use the Free plan for commercial websites, apps, and business projects, including in production" ([pricing](https://www.geoapify.com/pricing/)). Its routing page says "you can store and redistribute your results" ([routing API](https://www.geoapify.com/routing-api/)), but the terms say nothing about caching **[unverified in the terms]**. The free plan needs a "Powered by Geoapify" link.
- **Last resort: self-host Valhalla** (MIT) for the regions in use. Expect about €9 to €17 a month on a Hetzner box in Europe, or $25 to $40 on Railway **[derived; tile sizes unverified]**, plus a monthly rebuild and one more server to keep alive. OSRM for the whole US would need about 23 GiB of RAM, roughly $230 a month on Railway **[derived]**.
- **Ruled out because we cache forever:** Mapbox, Google Routes, HERE, TomTom, Stadia, GraphHopper's hosted API, LocationIQ's free plan and Amazon Location. Each one bans keeping results on our server or allows only 48 hours to 30 days (§2).

## 1. Today: the OSRM demo server

- `gitaway/geo.py` asks `router.project-osrm.org` for every new leg and caches the answer per family (`geo_cache`, kind `drive`).
- The OSRM project's page says the demo server's use "is restricted to reasonable, non-commercial use-cases. Do not exceed 1 request per second. We provide no guarantees wrt. uptime, latency, or data updates." It is FOSSGIS's server, also reachable as `routing.openstreetmap.de` ([OSRM wiki](https://github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/wiki/Demo-server), [about](https://routing.openstreetmap.de/about.html)).
- The full FOSSGIS policy is the English half of [this page](https://fossgis.de/arbeitsgruppen/osm-server/nutzungsbedingungen/). Its "Prohibited Uses" section says: "Commercial use is only permitted if the use of the services does not constitute a substantial part of an online offering. Example of a non-substantial part: Directions sketches on websites." and "Websites with high traffic volumes are generally not permitted to use our services." It also requires a link to openstreetmap.org/fixthemap and an identifying User-Agent, and says "Scripts are limited to one connection" and "Maximum one request per second". It recommends that "The URLs of our services should not be hardcoded into the app". FOSSGIS may "discontinue services at any time", and "each service is backed by only a single server".
- GitAway shows drive times in Today's "Leave by" and route lines on the Map tab, and now earns affiliate commission, so we don't qualify.
- **A bug, whatever we pick:** `OSRM_GATE = Gate(0.25)` allows four calls a second. Its comment says "the public router has no stated limit", but the limit is stated: one a second.
- `docs/design/cockpit/tools/fetch_geo.py` (map data for the designs) calls the same server, and F-201 plans to make that script "part of the app". It should switch to the chosen router too.

**More on openrouteservice:**
- The host moved. HeiGIT is "deprecating the URL api.openrouteservice.org in favour of api.heigit.org". From 27 August 2026 the old URL got "only 10% of the quota", and it was due to shut down "November 02 - 06, 2026". Directions now live at `api.heigit.org/openrouteservice/v2/directions` ([announcement](https://ask.openrouteservice.org/t/deprecating-api-openrouteservice-org-in-favour-of-api-heigit-org/7912), [quota cut](https://ask.openrouteservice.org/t/reducing-the-quota-of-deprecated-api-api-openrouteservice-org/8013)). Use the new host from day one.
- Over the daily limit you get "403 - Forbidden", over the per-minute limit "429 - Too many requests" ([FAQ](https://giscience.github.io/openrouteservice/frequently-asked-questions)). The `geo.py` gate already backs off for ten minutes on both. "If you repeatedly exceed the limits, your access to the API will be temporarily blocked" ([terms](https://account.heigit.org/info/tos)).
- "an API key must not be used client-side in an application" ([FAQ](https://giscience.github.io/openrouteservice/frequently-asked-questions)). We only call it from the server.
- The fine print: "Any person is permitted one single account for all HeiGIT APIs and services". Terms changes are "effective immediately upon posting" ([terms](https://account.heigit.org/info/tos)). No uptime promise appears anywhere I read.
- The terms forbid sending personal data ("Transmit personal data (apart from the data required by the HeiGIT account management)"). We send only coordinate pairs, as today (docs/family-db.md, "What leaves the server").
- Share-alike: a route line we publish, for example on a creator's public trip page (F-205), carries CC BY-SA and the credit. My reading **[unverified]** is that this reaches the route data, not our pages or code. A family's private trip isn't "Share" in the licence's sense ("to provide material to the public"), but showing the credit everywhere is simplest.

### Map data

### Memory and disk

- **OSRM** ([requirements](https://github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/wiki/Disk-and-Memory-Requirements), planet of November 2021 at 61 GiB): building the car profile peaks at 415 GiB of RAM (extract). Serving needs "around 123 GiB of RAM", plus "about 300 GB disk space". "The sizes scale roughly linear with input data size", and the foot profile is bigger than car. By that ratio, the US car profile needs about 78 GiB to build and about 23 GiB to serve; California alone about 8 GiB and 2.4 GiB **[derived from 2021 ratios]**.
- **GraphHopper** ([deploy guide](https://github.com/graphhopper/graphhopper/blob/master/docs/core/deploy.md)): a world import needs "~60GB RAM" and about 3 hours (31 GB with memory mapping, but "3 days!"). CH for car with turn costs needs "~120GB RAM" and about 25 hours. North America is 86M of the world's 415M edges (May 2022), so about 13 GB to import and 25 GB for CH **[derived]**.
- **Valhalla:** the graph is cut into tiles so you can "impose limits on how much of the graph resides in memory at any one time" ([why tiles](https://github.com/valhalla/valhalla/blob/master/docs/docs/concepts/why-tiles.md)). The default `max_cache_size` is 1,000,000,000, the "Number of bytes per thread used to store tile data in memory" ([valhalla_build_config](https://github.com/valhalla/valhalla/blob/master/scripts/valhalla_build_config)). On building, a maintainer said "it shouldnt need that much ram but it will use as much ram as it can (it memory maps files that are many 10s of gigabytes)", and another that their planet "conversions are running on tasks that have 16GB of RAM" ([issue 2384](https://github.com/valhalla/valhalla/issues/2384), 2020). The Docker maintainer says "the runtime for the service requires much less memory" than building ([discussion 90](https://github.com/gis-ops/docker-valhalla/discussions/90)). Valhalla publishes no tile sizes for the US **[unverified]**. The official image `ghcr.io/valhalla/valhalla-scripted` builds tiles from a `tile_urls` PBF link and "Supports auto rebuild on OSM file changes through md5" ([Docker README](https://github.com/valhalla/valhalla/blob/master/docker/README.md)).

## 4. Recommendation for a solo founder

1. **Switch to openrouteservice's Standard plan now.** It's the only option that is free, explicitly open to everyone, licenses its results so we may keep them forever, covers the world, and has a walking profile. At our volume the quota is irrelevant. The risk is that HeiGIT changes quotas or terms (it already cut the old host's quota to 10% to push a migration), and there's no uptime promise. Our cache softens both: a leg is asked once per family, and a failed call just leaves the time blank.
2. **Keep Geoapify Free as the fallback.** It's a different URL and parser behind the same `_leg` function, with its own key. Switch if HeiGIT changes its terms, or if we outgrow 2,000 legs a day.
3. **Self-host Valhalla only if** both free tiers change terms, we need an uptime guarantee, or volume reaches tens of thousands of new legs a month. Start with a Hetzner CX33 in Europe (€8.99) holding the US plus any active regions, and build the tiles on the founder's machine.
4. **Don't wait on the demo server.** The policy is plain, FOSSGIS can cut access without notice, and our gate breaks its rate limit today.

## 5. Code-change note for `gitaway/geo.py`

- **URL:** `ROUTER = "https://api.heigit.org/openrouteservice/v2/directions/{profile}?start={lon1},{lat1}&end={lon2},{lat2}"`. The GET form takes `start` and `end` as `longitude,latitude` and answers GeoJSON ([RoutingAPI.java](https://github.com/GIScience/openrouteservice/blob/main/ors-api/src/main/java/org/heigit/ors/api/controllers/RoutingAPI.java)). Profile `driving-car` now, `foot-walking` later.
- **Key:** read it from the env var **`ORS_API_KEY`** (the name only; set it on Railway and list it in docs/setup.md). The docs pass it as `?api_key=` ([v2 examples](https://github.com/GIScience/openrouteservice/blob/main/docs/versions/v7.1.1/documentation/Switching-from-V1-API-to-V2.md)). An `Authorization` header probably works too and keeps the key out of URLs **[unverified for GET]**; that would mean `fetch()` taking an optional headers dict. Never log the URL.
- **No key** (dev, CI): `_leg` returns None, so no drive times, and it never falls back to the demo server. Tests already replace `fetch`.
- **Gate:** `ROUTER_GATE = Gate(1.5)`, which is exactly 40 a minute, the Standard limit. The ten-minute back-off on 403 and 429 stays.
- **Parsing:** take `f = r["features"][0]`. Minutes are `max(1, round(f["properties"]["summary"]["duration"] / 60))` and the line is `f["geometry"]`, a LineString of `[lon, lat]` like OSRM's ([return types](https://giscience.github.io/openrouteservice/api-reference/endpoints/directions/requests-and-return-types)). The cached `{"m", "g"}` shape stays the same.
- **Cache:** the `drive|lat,lon;lat,lon` keys stay, so legs already cached from OSRM remain. OpenStreetMap data may be kept with credit ([openstreetmap.org/copyright](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright): "You are free to copy, distribute, transmit and adapt our data, as long as you credit OpenStreetMap and its contributors"), and FOSSGIS's policy is about using its server, not keeping its answers **[my reading]**. Walking gets its own kind (`walk|…`) so a walk never reads a drive.
- **Credit:** add "Routes © openrouteservice by HeiGIT" to the Map tab's Leaflet attribution, next to the existing OpenStreetMap credit. Replace "OSRM (router.project-osrm.org) works out drives" on /privacy (`gitaway/pages/legal.py`) and the OSRM line in docs/family-db.md ("What leaves the server") with HeiGIT's openrouteservice.
- **Tests:** `tests/test_geo.py` and `tests/test_tripgeo.py` fake OSRM's answer and look for "router.project-osrm" in URLs. They switch to openrouteservice's host and answer shape.
- **Ready for the fallback:** keep the provider in two small functions, one building the URL and one parsing the answer. Then Geoapify is a swap: `https://api.geoapify.com/v1/routing?waypoints={lat1},{lon1}|{lat2},{lon2}&mode=drive&apiKey=…` (note latitude first), with the key in `GEOAPIFY_API_KEY`. Its time is `properties.time` in seconds, and its geometry is a MultiLineString with one line per leg, so take `coordinates[0]` ([docs](https://apidocs.geoapify.com/docs/routing/)).

## 6. Not covered here

- `geo.py`'s geocoder (Nominatim) and the Map tab's tiles (tile.openstreetmap.org) have their own usage policies. FOSSGIS's page notes that the OpenStreetMap Foundation's tile server is a separate service. A commercial app should check both in their own ticket.
- Live traffic: the free OpenStreetMap-based options time drives from the road network without live traffic **[unverified per provider]**. Traffic-aware times (Mapbox `driving-traffic`, HERE time-aware routing, Google's `TRAFFIC_AWARE`) come only from services ruled out above.
