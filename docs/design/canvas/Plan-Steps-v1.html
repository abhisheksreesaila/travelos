STEPS INSIDE A PLAN · ONE DISNEYLAND DAY

A park day you can follow step by step

On a calendar, "Disneyland, 8 to 9:30" looks fine, but it doesn't say what to do. Here the block opens into the morning, the afternoon and the evening, then each ride or meal, then the one thing in front of you. Who goes where is in colour, and the family hears about every change. The flight home rides along: seats, gate and boarding passes in one place. Ride times and Lightning Lane windows, the flight, seats and confirmation are samples.

On the laptop, the whole day at once

The same Disneyland block on the desktop calendar opens wide: the day's parts and every step in lanes, with the chosen step beside it. Planning at a desk works; the phone is where the day is lived.

What we'd build first

In the order a family would notice it.

  • 1
    Steps inside plansAny plan opens into parts of the day and steps, each with a time, who it's for (everyone, adults, kids) and a note. Split steps sit side by side in lanes.
  • 2
    Touch editingHold and drag to move a step, swipe for Done or Remove, and add one with who chips and a time wheel.
  • 3
    Paste a list and dictationPaste lines from Notes or a text, or speak with the iPhone keyboard mic. You see the steps before anything is added.
  • 4
    Passes & documentsSeats, groups, gate and the boarding pass for each person, in Help, for the family only, and full-screen at the gate.
  • 5
    Notifications for step changesWhen someone adds, moves or finishes a step, the family's phones say exactly what changed, and the thread keeps a card of it.
LATER, WITH THE ASSISTANT

Free-speech changes: "swap Dumbo for the teacups and push lunch to 12:30" becomes a proposed change you can see and accept, the same way Ask will work. Until then, dictation follows the simple pattern: time, what, who.
