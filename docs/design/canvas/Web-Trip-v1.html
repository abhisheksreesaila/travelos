The web gets the same love

Three ways to lay out the trip on a laptop. Each frame is the real size of the screen at 100% zoom (1440×900, one at 1280×800), shrunk to fit here. Click Actual size to see it as it will look.

A

Grid + docked panel

Brief's pick

One top bar (Trip · Family · Ask) on every signed-in page. The day sits on the left; whatever you click opens on the right instead of over the day. The panel always holds something useful: the clicked plan, the family chat, or Ask with "Up next".

A1 · a plan clicked: its card, steps and chat on the right · hover shows the ⋯ menu and the resize knob · 1440×900
A2 · nothing clicked (the evening check during the trip): Up next and Ask · a new plan being dragged out on the grid (11 – 12) · 1440×900
A3 · Family tab: every chat on the trip, docked · 1280×800 (smaller laptop)
A4 · Week: the whole trip at a glance, titles on one or two lines, click a day to open it · 1440×900
  • Keyboard: T today, ← → days, W / D week or day, / Ask, Esc closes the panel back to Up next.
  • Mouse: drag empty time to make a plan, drag a plan to move it, pull the knob to resize, right-click or ⋯ to rename or delete.
  • The panel slides between Plan, Family and Ask with the same liquid motion as the phone; the grid never jumps.
  • Below about 1100 wide the panel becomes the phone's sheet again; the phone app is unchanged.
B

Week rail + grid + panel

Same as A, with the five days always listed down the left so you can see the whole trip while you plan one day. Pro: planning before the trip is fastest. Con: three columns are tighter; the grid and panel each lose width, and on a 1280 laptop it gets cramped.

B1 · 1440×900
C

Slim sidebar instead of a top bar

Like A, but Trip, Family, Ask and settings live in a narrow icon bar on the left, so the day gets the full height (about one extra hour of grid). Pro: most room for the day; feels like a Mac app. Con: the old site pages (Community, Plan a trip) would need the same sidebar, a bigger change than a top bar.
