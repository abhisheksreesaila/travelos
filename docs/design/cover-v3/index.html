The globe of shared trips

A small low-poly globe turns slowly; every pin is a guide someone shared.

Scroll flies one trip out: matrix squares light along its arc, your people hop on from their own cities.

It lands as a guide pinned to the globe, and the globe fills with trips.

Why it carries the soul

The loop is literal: trips leave, come home, and seed the next family's trip. The network is something you see grow.

Risks

  • Weight: one canvas, 1,280 flat faces, drawn only while on screen; fine on a mid phone.
  • Motion sickness: low. Slow spin, no camera fly-through; reduced motion stops it.
  • Mobile: globe up top, words in a card; small arcs can read as abstract.
  • A globe is a familiar travel cliché; the arcs and avatars must carry the "together".

One postcard's journey

One shared guide, as a postcard, is the whole story.

It unfolds into the matrix like a map, your people stick notes on it, and it wears the trip's skies as it happens.

Stamped and shared, it joins a wall of thousands, where another family picks it up.

Why it carries the soul

Tactile and warm; giving back is a physical act (your card goes on the wall). Closest to the guides prototype's paper and sky.

Risks

  • Weight: the lightest (plus 7 KB shared page script, as for all three): DOM and CSS 3D only; the wall is ~230 gradient tiles.
  • Motion sickness: lowest. One object moves; the camera never does.
  • Mobile: the map unfolds one way (two panels), three days of the matrix.
  • A flipping, folding card can feel like a gimmick if the beats are slow.

The tabletop world

A miniature table you can nudge: the matrix is a board of tiles, each with its tiny weather.

Your people are pieces that hop onto the pick; a creator's piece brings a guide.

A tiny plane flies off and comes home to drop a guidebook on the shared shelf; neighbours borrow it.

Why it carries the soul

Planning as play, together: the most "not stressful" of the three. The shelf is give-and-take in one picture.

Risks

  • Weight: ~150 CSS 3D faces and a tilt-shift blur: the heaviest DOM option; the blur goes on low-end phones.
  • Motion sickness: low; the table only turns when you drag it.
  • Mobile: tiny type on tiles; the drag is sideways only so it never fights scrolling.
  • "Game night" could feel too cute for a $2,000 decision.
